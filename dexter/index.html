About DEXTER

DEXTER is a pilot study exploring how sublingual dexmedetomidine (SL Dex) influences circadian rhythms, sleep architecture, and the daily pattern of blood biomarkers. We are particularly interested in plasma phosphorylated tau 217 (p-tau217) and how its levels vary across the 24-hour sleep–wake cycle.

The development of a sublingual formulation, administered under the tongue, provides a noninvasive way to study dexmedetomidine beyond the intensive care settings commonly associated with intravenous administration. Preliminary studies suggest that SL Dex may increase slow-wave activity during sleep, shorten the time it takes to fall asleep, and prolong the time before rapid eye movement (REM) sleep begins. Its effects on circadian regulation and the timing of tau biomarker changes in humans remain to be systematically characterized.

DEXTER will examine whether SL Dex produces measurable changes in sleep and circadian physiology alongside changes in tau biomarker dynamics. The findings will help inform future studies of how pharmacologic modulation of sleep and circadian biology relates to aging and neurobiology.

Study Approach

Much of the prior clinical research on dexmedetomidine has taken place during surgery or in other medically complex settings. Pain, inflammation, other medications, disrupted environments, and irregular daily routines can make it difficult to distinguish the effects of the drug from other influences on sleep and circadian rhythms.

DEXTER uses a carefully controlled laboratory protocol that standardizes light exposure, posture, feeding, activity, and the timing of sampling. This approach is designed to reduce environmental and behavioral confounding and characterize the physiologic effects of SL Dex across the sleep–wake cycle.

What we will study
  • Circadian regulation: how SL Dex influences the body's daily rhythms.
  • Sleep architecture: changes in the organization and timing of sleep stages, including slow-wave activity and the time to sleep and REM onset.
  • Biomarker dynamics: the temporal profile of tau biomarkers, particularly plasma p-tau217, over 24 hours.

By examining these outcomes together, DEXTER aims to clarify whether sleep-like sedation is accompanied by measurable sleep and circadian effects and changes in tau dynamics.
